## LinkedIn

Between 30 September and 4 October, a slow-moving low-pressure system over the southeast Mediterranean brought five days of heavy rain to Crete. Stations of the National Observatory of Athens (meteo.gr) recorded over 840 mm at Samaria (Xyloskalo), over 640 mm at Anogeia and 448 mm at Tzermiado on the Lasithi Plateau. meteo.gr describes it as the most significant storm in Crete in at least 25 years.

How much is that? These stations have kept records since 2006-2008. At Samaria and Anogeia, these five days brought more rain than any single month in their records. At all three, it was between 45% and 65% of an average year: months of rain in five days.

Our thoughts are with the family of the person who lost their life, and with everyone affected across the island.

The contrast with the recent past is stark. In 2025 several Cretan municipalities were under water-scarcity emergencies, and in December 2025 the Aposelemis reservoir that supplies Heraklion was nearly empty. During this storm it increased from 9.3 to 12.7 million m³. Drought and flood on the same island, months apart: this is the range of water extremes SEASONEXT works on.

What forecasts can and cannot do: no seasonal forecast can predict a single storm weeks ahead. What seasonal forecasts can do is shift the odds for the coming months (wetter or drier, warmer or cooler than normal). Turning those odds into information that water managers in Crete can use is the aim of SEASONEXT.

Data: NOA / meteo.gr station network (event totals preliminary; station records 2006-2025). Map: CLIMADAT-Grid, Varotsos et al. (2025), Earth System Science Data.

## Alt text

Map of Crete shaded by regular annual rainfall, with three stations marked: Samaria, Anogeia and Tzermiado. Below, bars compare five-day rain with each station's average year: Samaria over 840 mm (about 55% of 1,540 mm), more than any month on record since 2008 (previous high 728 mm); Anogeia over 640 mm (about 65% of 990 mm), more than any month since 2008 (previous high 454 mm); Tzermiado 448 mm (about 45% of 1,020 mm), below its wettest month on record (663 mm).
