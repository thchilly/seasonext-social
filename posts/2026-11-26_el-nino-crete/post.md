# El Niño is back. What it means, and doesn't, for Crete

## LinkedIn

El Niño is now strong and still growing. What does it mean for winter rain in Crete? Very little, as far as the record shows.

El Niño is a warming of the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean that lasts for months. Because the ocean changes slowly, it is the main source of skill in seasonal forecasts worldwide, shifting rain and temperature in the tropics and beyond in fairly predictable ways.

The current event is one of the strongest since 1950. NOAA's El Niño index (the Relative Oceanic Niño Index, RONI) was +2.0 °C for August to October 2026, the second highest for that time of year after 1997. On 12 November, NOAA's Climate Prediction Center gave about a 60% chance that the event will become the strongest since 1950 by late autumn and early winter, and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) expects a peak around December. These are forecasts.

Far from the Pacific, the signal fades. The figure compares winter rain over Crete (December to February, ERA5) with the strength of El Niño or La Niña in each of the last 76 winters. The correlation is -0.12: El Niño explains about 1% of the ups and downs of Crete's winter rain, too little to tell apart from chance. The seven winters with a strong El Niño ranged from the 21st wettest (1972-73) to the driest of all (2015-16). Five of the seven were drier than the middle winter, but seven cases are too few for a rule. A review of the research (Brönnimann 2007) also finds that El Niño's effects on European climate change with the season and from one event to the next.

So a strong El Niño does not tell us whether this winter in Crete will be wet or dry. Our monthly Forecast vs reality posts keep checking the seasonal forecasts, which combine many such signals, against what happens.

Graphic in the "Field news" series, titled "A strong El Niño says little about winter rain in Crete". A scatter plot of 76 winters, 1950-51 to 2025-26: winter (December to February) rain over Crete from ERA5, from about 130 to 430 mm, against NOAA's El Niño index for that winter, from about -2 (La Niña) to +2.4 (El Niño). ## Sources and checks

- NOAA Climate Prediction Center, ENSO Diagnostic Discussion of 12 November 2026 (El Niño Advisory): strong-to-very strong El Niño likely through January-March 2027 (greater than 79%); chances of a historic event exceeding all El Niños since 1950 (3-month RONI of +2.5 °C or more) about 60% for October-December and November-January. Next discussion 10 December. https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml
- RONI, 3-month values: https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/data/indices/RONI.ascii.txt. August-October 2026: +1.97 (rounded to +2.0). Highest August-October values since 1950: 1997 +2.07, 2026 +1.97, 2015 +1.80. CPC uses RONI for official monitoring since February 2026. Other indices give higher values (traditional ONI, August-October 2026: +2.46; weekly Niño 3.4 in the CPC text: +2.5); the post uses RONI, the lower and official one.
- WMO Global Seasonal Climate Update, 21 October 2026: peak expected around December 2026.
- Crete winter rain: ERA5 monthly means, Crete land points (land-sea mask above 0.5, cosine-latitude weights, the same box and method as the monthly checks), December-February summed, 76 winters 1950-51 to 2025-26. Middle (median) winter 269 mm; range 131 to 433 mm.
- Correlation with the December-February RONI: -0.117 (Pearson; Spearman -0.09); explained share r² = 1.4%, "about 1%". Permutation test (20,000 shuffles): p = 0.32, "too little to tell apart from chance".
- Strong El Niño winters (DJF RONI 1.5 or more), RONI, rain, rank of 76 (1 = wettest): 1982-83 2.40, 257 mm, 43rd; 2015-16 2.14, 131 mm, 76th (driest); 1997-98 2.13, 211 mm, 62nd; 1991-92 2.12, 288 mm, 31st; 1957-58 1.89, 179 mm, 72nd; 1972-73 1.69, 304 mm, 21st; 1965-66 1.53, 259 mm, 41st. Five of seven below the median (ranks above 38).
- Brönnimann, S. (2007): Impact of El Niño-Southern Oscillation on European climate. Reviews of Geophysics 45, RG3003, doi:10.1029/2006RG000199. Cited for: effects exist but are seasonally modulated and nonlinear.
- "Main source of skill in seasonal forecasts worldwide": standard statement in seasonal forecasting (C3S and ECMWF documentation).
- ERA5 before 1979 has fewer observations behind it; for the satellite era alone (47 winters, 1979-80 to 2025-26) the correlation is -0.09, so the conclusion does not depend on the early years.
- No forecast for Crete is given and nothing is presented as a warning. No project results; public data processed with the published method of the monthly checks.
