## LinkedIn

Two weeks after the storm that hit Crete in early October, the reservoir that supplies Heraklion is close to full again.

On 30 September, the Aposelemis reservoir held 9.3 million m³ of water, a little over a third of its capacity of about 25 million m³. In December 2025 it had been described as nearly empty, after a year of water-scarcity emergencies in parts of the island.

According to the Crete Development Organisation (OAK), which operates the dam, storage reached 12.7 million m³ on 4 October and 17.6 million m³ (70%) on 8 October. By 16 October, press reports put it above 20.3 million m³, over 85% full. The reservoir last overflowed in February 2022.

Why did it keep rising after the rain stopped? A reservoir collects the runoff of its whole catchment. After intense rain, streams and the flooded Lasithi Plateau keep draining for days, so storage rises long after the storm has passed.

Whether this water lasts through next summer depends on the coming winter's rain and on demand. Planning for that, months ahead, is where seasonal forecasts can help, and it is the question SEASONEXT works on for water managers in Crete.

Data: storage as announced by OAK (via politica.gr, neakriti and LatoNews); 16 October value from neakriti.
