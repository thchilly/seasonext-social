## LinkedIn

After the storm that hit Crete in early October, a logical question is whether it could have been seen coming months ahead. It could not, and that is not what seasonal forecasts are for.

A weather forecast answers a question like: will it rain in Heraklion on Saturday, and how much? It looks up to a few days ahead and describes specific events: timing, place, amount. It is what warnings are built on.

A seasonal forecast answers a different question: will the coming months be drier, normal or wetter than usual? It looks one to seven months ahead and gives odds, not dates. In early August, for example, the forecasts gave a 49% chance of a wetter than normal September in Crete, against 33% if nothing were known.

Why the difference? Day-to-day weather is chaotic and loses its predictability after about two weeks. What stays predictable for months are the slow parts of the climate system, such as ocean temperatures, which shift the odds for a whole season without fixing any single day.

Weather forecasts warn about the storm. Seasonal forecasts help plan the season: reservoir operations, irrigation, water restrictions. SEASONEXT works on the second, for water managers in Crete.

SEASONEXT is led by Hydromech, the Hydrology and Hydraulic Engineering Laboratory, TUC.

## X and Bluesky

Could the early-October storm in Crete have been forecast months ahead? No. Weather forecasts describe events up to about two weeks ahead; seasonal forecasts give the odds of a drier or wetter season, one to seven months ahead. Different questions, both useful.
